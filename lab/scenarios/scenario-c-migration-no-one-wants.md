# Scenario C — The Migration No One Wants

**Hand this to the pair. The technical steps in the worksheet are the same for everyone — this scenario shapes how you frame your demo.**

---

**Customer:** Enterprise financial services firm, ~12,000 employees, multiple business units, highly regulated.

**Their stack:**
- Mix of on-prem and cloud — Oracle, Teradata, some Snowflake (one BU), legacy Tableau Server (org-wide), Power BI (another BU), Excel everywhere
- Strong central IT/security org. Light analytics engineering function. No standardized data engineering practices across BUs.
- GitHub Enterprise exists, but adoption varies wildly by BU

**Their problem:** Central IT wants to consolidate to one analytics platform. Three BUs have opinions. The CIO has heard the pitch and wants to "see what modern looks like." They are not sold.

**Your champion:** None yet. You're talking to the central data platform team — engineers, not analysts.

**Their concern, in their words:**
> "We don't have analytics engineers. We have 800 Tableau users and an Excel-first culture. What do you actually expect a company like ours to do with Git and a CLI? Be honest."

---

**For your demo, be ready to answer:**
- This pair's challenge is positioning, not just building. When does the Git/CLI story land for this customer, and when is it a distraction?
- Where do you *lead* with the dev workflow, and where do you *hide* it?
- How do you answer "be honest" honestly — without either overselling the modern-stack story or abandoning it?

**What this tests:** Reading the room. Persona awareness. Knowing when the dev story helps and when it hurts — the exact thread that surfaced in the Module 1 discussion.
